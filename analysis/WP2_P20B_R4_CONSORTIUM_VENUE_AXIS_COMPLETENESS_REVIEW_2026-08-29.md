# WP2-P20B-R4 — Consortium Venue-Axis Completeness Review

Date: 2026-08-29
Status: **PASS / VENUE-DECISION VECTOR COMPLETE / NO SCIENTIFIC REOPENING**

## Trigger

The author asked whether additional venue-selection axes remain before selecting a journal. A bounded consortium-style review was run across publication strategy, editorial fit, bibliometrics/promotion, industrial translation, open science/reproducibility, rights/IP, and research-strategy perspectives.

The consortium explicitly separated:

- **decision axes** — dimensions that can materially change venue ranking;
- **due-diligence gates** — conditions that must pass but should not be given artificial numerical weight when all candidates are established serious venues.

## Final decision vector — 8 axes only

### A1 — Scientific fit & editorial/reviewer plausibility

Questions:
- Does the current manuscript naturally belong in the venue?
- Does the venue publish similar current work?
- Can editors/reviewers evaluate the actual contribution without demanding a different paper?
- What is the desk-reject/reviewer-expertise risk?

This remains the primary scientific axis.

### A2 — Academic / institutional recognition & field signaling

This is distinct from generic prestige.

For the Egyptian context, current SCU guidance makes Web of Science/Scopus subject classification and year-specific journal status materially relevant to academic evaluation. Current SCU cycle-15 guidance also gives special treatment to qualifying WoS Q1/Q2 papers in the main indices.

Questions:
- Is the venue indexed and recognized in the required databases in the relevant year/category?
- How does it signal the author's actual field identity: IoT, networking, systems, engineering, or broad multidisciplinary science?
- Does the venue strengthen promotion/institutional value without distorting the paper?

### A3 — Industrial Pull / Translational Discoverability

Questions:
- Are corporate R&D, technology scouts, patent teams, standards engineers, product architects, TTOs and venture builders likely to search/read this ecosystem?
- Does the publisher/platform connect naturally to engineering standards, patents, corporate R&D workflows, or applied-technology discovery?

This axis was introduced in P20B-R3.

### A4 — Audience reach, access & citation discoverability

This is not identical to industrial pull.

Questions:
- Is the work open or easily shareable?
- Is the platform strong for academic search/discovery across the intended community?
- Does the venue provide broad reach when cross-disciplinary exposure is useful?
- Will the paper be easy to find by future collaborators, students and grant partners?

Example distinction: Scientific Reports offers very broad open-access reach; IEEE offers narrower but highly concentrated engineering discovery.

### A5 — Economics & route certainty

Questions:
- APC or subscription route?
- National/institutional publishing agreement?
- Page/overlength/color/supplement charges?
- Taxes and residual charges?
- Is coverage guaranteed, conditional, or unknown?

P20B-R2 established the mandatory publishing-agreement gate.

### A6 — Speed & process predictability

Speed must not be reduced to a publisher-advertised mean.

Questions:
- first-decision / acceptance / online-publication time;
- desk-reject probability and editorial throughput risk;
- reviewer-availability risk;
- whether the process is predictable enough for the publication objective.

Fast but highly volatile editorial routing is not equivalent to a predictably fast route.

### A7 — Rights, IP/control & commercialization optionality

Questions:
- preprint policy;
- accepted-manuscript self-archiving;
- embargo length;
- copyright/license options;
- author retention of patent/trademark rights;
- commercial-reuse implications;
- ability to preserve a later licensing/startup/standards option.

This is separate from APC economics. A cheap venue can still impose less attractive sharing/reuse conditions.

P20C must still perform the pre-publication IP screen before public disclosure if potentially protectable foreground IP exists.

### A8 — Evidence-package preservation & reproducibility

Questions:
- page/word limits;
- figure/table constraints;
- supplementary-material support;
- software/data/artifact support;
- repository links and data-availability statements;
- whether current P18RC/P19 evidence architecture survives without destructive compression.

This axis protects scientific integrity against venue-driven formatting distortion.

## Tiebreaker — strategic option value

Future grants, collaborators, standards activity, keynote/invitation potential, recruitment and follow-on-paper positioning are real benefits, but the consortium does **not** recommend a ninth weighted axis. These effects mostly flow from A2–A4 and would otherwise be double-counted.

Use **Strategic Option Value** only as a tiebreaker between venues with materially similar 8-axis scores.

## Due-diligence gates — not weighted axes

The following remain mandatory pass/fail checks but should not receive independent scoring weight unless a specific candidate creates a real problem:

- journal legitimacy / predatory-journal screen;
- COPE/publication-ethics compliance;
- AI-assisted-writing policy compatibility;
- plagiarism/originality policy;
- data/privacy/security restrictions;
- accessibility requirements;
- correction/retraction/post-publication policy;
- digital preservation/archival permanence;
- special-issue/guest-editor risk;
- sanctions/export/legal constraints where applicable;
- authorship/COI/funding disclosure compatibility.

## Current official-policy evidence affecting the framework

### SCU academic-value axis

Current SCU guidance states that international-journal classification is determined for the publication/acceptance year and selected from Web of Science and Scopus subject classifications according to the researcher's precise/general field and committee. Current cycle-15 (2025–2028) FAQ also states that papers in WoS Q1/Q2 journals in SCIE/AHCI/SSCI receive plagiarism-check exemption, while ESCI does not receive that exemption.

This makes A2 materially decision-relevant for an Egypt-based academic publication strategy.

### Rights/self-archiving axis

Current IEEE policy allows preprints on approved nonprofit servers such as arXiv/TechRxiv, institutional repositories and personal sites, and distinguishes preprint/accepted/version-of-record sharing.

Current Springer Nature policy permits self-archiving of accepted manuscripts under route-specific embargo rules and states that authors retain patent and trademark rights under its standard licence structures.

Current Elsevier policies distinguish OA and subscription licences and allow accepted-manuscript repository sharing subject to journal-specific embargo terms.

Thus A7 is a real venue discriminator rather than a theoretical concern.
