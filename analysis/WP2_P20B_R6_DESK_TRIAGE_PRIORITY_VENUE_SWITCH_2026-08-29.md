# WP2-P20B-R6 — Desk-Triage Priority Venue Switch

Date: 2026-08-29
Status: **PASS / FIRST VENUE SWITCHED / NO SCIENTIFIC REOPENING**

## Trigger

The author identified a material objective that had been underweighted in the eight-axis venue model: **time to editorial triage / desk acceptance-rejection**, not merely average time to first peer-review decision.

This creates a legitimate venue-selection reweighting. Science, novelty boundaries, authorship, rights, IP state, experiments and evidence remain frozen.

## Current evidence

### Internet of Things (Elsevier)

Official current Elsevier scope explicitly covers:

- unique IoT challenges in reliability;
- IoT software development and engineering;
- testbeds and quality assurance;
- full research papers;
- survey papers;
- open software and data;
- researchers, engineers and practitioners;
- a stated **high priority on timely publication**.

The journal was already the #2 route under the frozen eight-axis R5 score at 89/100, only one point behind IEEE IoT-J.

Current secondary reproduction of Elsevier Journal Insights (checked 2026-08-29) reports approximately **4 days submission to first decision**, 54 days to decision after review, 116 days to acceptance and 6 days acceptance-to-online. The exact 4-day figure is treated as **secondary evidence**, not a guaranteed SLA, because the directly accessible official Elsevier page currently exposes the timely-publication commitment and scope but not the full Journal Insights timing panel through this retrieval channel.

### IEEE Internet of Things Journal

IEEE IoT-J remains a strong specialist scientific/industrial venue, but the official average of 6.9 weeks to first decision does not separately expose desk-triage latency. Under the author's revised objective, that uncertainty carries more weight.

## Decision

The first-submission route is switched to:

**Internet of Things (Elsevier)**

Initial publishing route:

**Subscription / non-OA**

IEEE Internet of Things Journal becomes the first backup route.

## Rationale

The switch is not based on publisher preference. It follows from:

1. near-tied overall scientific/strategic score before the switch;
2. explicit author priority for fast desk/editorial triage;
3. direct scope match for reliability, testbeds, quality assurance, and applied IoT engineering;
4. greater manuscript-space flexibility, allowing the structured literature survey and evidence effort to remain visible rather than compressed by the IEEE eight-page cost threshold;
5. strong practitioner/corporate-R&D discoverability retained through the Elsevier/ScienceDirect ecosystem.
